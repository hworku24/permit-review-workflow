# City of Rivermont Municipal Code, Title 59: Zoning

Adopted March 4, 2019. Amended through June 2, 2026.

Rivermont is a fictional jurisdiction. These provisions are written to resemble the
structure and language of a mid-size US zoning ordinance so the retrieval layer has
realistic material to work against. They are not the law anywhere.

## § 59-1.2.1 Purpose and applicability

This Title governs the use, bulk, height, and placement of structures within the City of
Rivermont. No building permit shall be issued for work that does not conform to this
Title, except where a variance has been granted by the Board of Zoning Appeals under
Article 9 or where the work qualifies as a lawful nonconforming use under § 59-8.1.1.

## § 59-1.3.4 Measurement of setbacks

A setback is measured as the shortest horizontal distance between the lot line and the
nearest point of the structure, including eaves, bay windows, and cantilevered elements
projecting more than twenty-four inches. Uncovered steps and landings not exceeding
thirty inches in height above grade are excluded from setback measurement.

## § 59-2.1.1 Residential districts established

The following residential districts are established: R-200, R-90, R-60, and RT-8. The
numeral in each district designation indicates the minimum lot area in hundreds of square
feet, except in the RT-8 district where the numeral indicates the maximum permitted
density in dwelling units per acre.

## § 59-2.2.3 R-60 district, dimensional standards

In the R-60 district the minimum lot area is 6,000 square feet and the minimum lot width
at the front building line is 60 feet. The minimum front setback is 25 feet. The minimum
side setback is 8 feet, and the sum of both side setbacks shall be not less than 20 feet.
The minimum rear setback is 25 feet. Maximum building height is 35 feet. Maximum lot
coverage by all structures is 35 percent.

## § 59-2.2.4 R-90 district, dimensional standards

In the R-90 district the minimum lot area is 9,000 square feet and the minimum lot width
at the front building line is 75 feet. The minimum front setback is 30 feet. The minimum
side setback is 10 feet, and the sum of both side setbacks shall be not less than 25 feet.
The minimum rear setback is 30 feet. Maximum building height is 35 feet. Maximum lot
coverage by all structures is 30 percent.

## § 59-2.2.5 R-200 district, dimensional standards

In the R-200 district the minimum lot area is 20,000 square feet and the minimum lot width
at the front building line is 100 feet. The minimum front setback is 50 feet. The minimum
side setback is 20 feet. The minimum rear setback is 50 feet. Maximum building height is
40 feet. Maximum lot coverage by all structures is 20 percent.

## § 59-2.2.6 RT-8 district, dimensional standards

In the RT-8 district the maximum density is 8 dwelling units per acre. The minimum front
setback is 20 feet and the minimum rear setback is 20 feet. No side setback is required
between attached units sharing a common party wall. An end unit shall provide a side
setback of not less than 10 feet. Maximum building height is 40 feet.

## § 59-2.4.1 Accessory structures, general

An accessory structure is a detached structure subordinate to the principal building and
located on the same lot. An accessory structure shall not be used as a dwelling unit
except where approved as an accessory dwelling unit under § 59-2.5.2.

## § 59-2.4.2 Accessory structures, placement

An accessory structure shall not be located within any required front setback. In
residential districts an accessory structure not exceeding 200 square feet in gross floor
area and not exceeding 12 feet in height may be located as close as 5 feet to a rear or
side lot line. An accessory structure exceeding either threshold shall comply with the
full setback requirements of the district.

## § 59-2.4.3 Accessory structures, aggregate limit

The aggregate gross floor area of all accessory structures on a residential lot shall not
exceed 900 square feet or 40 percent of the gross floor area of the principal dwelling,
whichever is less.

## § 59-2.5.2 Accessory dwelling units

One accessory dwelling unit is permitted per lot in the R-200, R-90, and R-60 districts.
The accessory dwelling unit shall not exceed 800 square feet of gross floor area or 35
percent of the gross floor area of the principal dwelling, whichever is less. One
additional off-street parking space is required unless the lot is within one-quarter mile
of a transit stop served at intervals of thirty minutes or less.

## § 59-2.6.1 Decks and uncovered platforms

An uncovered deck attached to a principal dwelling may project up to 12 feet into a
required rear setback provided that no part of the deck is closer than 15 feet to the rear
lot line. A deck exceeding 30 inches in height above grade at any point shall be included
in lot coverage calculations. A deck 30 inches or less in height above grade is excluded
from lot coverage.

## § 59-2.7.1 Fences and walls

In residential districts a fence or wall within a required front setback shall not exceed
4 feet in height. Elsewhere on the lot a fence or wall shall not exceed 6.5 feet in
height. A retaining wall exceeding 4 feet in exposed height requires sealed structural
plans under Title 60.

## § 59-3.1.1 Commercial districts established

The following commercial districts are established: C-1 Neighborhood Commercial and C-2
General Commercial. The MXD Mixed Use District is established under Article 4.

## § 59-3.2.2 C-1 district, dimensional standards

In the C-1 district the minimum lot area is 8,000 square feet. The minimum front setback
is 15 feet. No side or rear setback is required where the abutting lot is in a commercial
district. Where a C-1 lot abuts a residential district, a side or rear setback of not less
than 25 feet is required. Maximum building height is 40 feet. Maximum lot coverage is 60
percent.

## § 59-3.2.3 C-2 district, dimensional standards

In the C-2 district there is no minimum lot area. The minimum front setback is 10 feet. No
side or rear setback is required where the abutting lot is in a commercial or industrial
district. Where a C-2 lot abuts a residential district, a side or rear setback of not less
than 40 feet is required and a buffer meeting § 59-6.2.1 shall be provided. Maximum
building height is 75 feet. Maximum lot coverage is 80 percent.

## § 59-3.4.1 Height exceptions

Rooftop mechanical equipment, elevator overruns, parapets not exceeding 4 feet, chimneys,
and screening enclosures may exceed the district height limit by not more than 15 feet
provided that the aggregate footprint of all such elements does not exceed 25 percent of
the roof area.

## § 59-4.1.2 MXD district, general

The MXD district permits residential dwelling units above ground floor commercial use. The
ground floor of any building fronting a designated Main Street frontage shall be occupied
by a permitted commercial use to a depth of not less than 30 feet from the front facade.
Residential use is not permitted on the ground floor within that depth.

## § 59-4.2.1 MXD district, dimensional standards

In the MXD district there is no minimum lot area and no minimum front setback. Maximum
building height is 65 feet, except within 60 feet of a residential district boundary where
maximum height is 45 feet. Maximum lot coverage is 85 percent.

## § 59-5.1.1 Off-street parking, residential

Two off-street parking spaces are required per single family detached dwelling. One space
is required per dwelling unit in the RT-8 district. Required parking shall not be located
within a required front setback except on a driveway serving the dwelling.

## § 59-5.1.4 Off-street parking, commercial

Off-street parking for commercial use shall be provided at a rate of one space per 300
square feet of gross floor area for retail use, one space per 250 square feet for office
use, and one space per 100 square feet of patron area for eating and drinking
establishments. A reduction of up to 25 percent may be approved where a shared parking
analysis under § 59-5.3.1 demonstrates that peak demands do not coincide.

## § 59-5.2.2 Bicycle parking

A commercial building exceeding 10,000 square feet of gross floor area shall provide
bicycle parking at a rate of one space per 5,000 square feet, with a minimum of four
spaces. Bicycle parking shall be located within 100 feet of a principal entrance.

## § 59-6.2.1 Buffers adjoining residential districts

Where a commercial or industrial use abuts a residential district, a landscaped buffer of
not less than 25 feet in width shall be provided along the shared lot line. The buffer
shall contain not fewer than 4 canopy trees and 12 shrubs per 100 linear feet, and shall
include an opaque screen of not less than 6 feet in height.

## § 59-6.3.1 Tree canopy requirement

A development site of one-half acre or larger shall achieve a minimum tree canopy coverage
of 20 percent at twenty year maturity in residential districts and 10 percent in
commercial and industrial districts. Existing trees of 24 inch diameter at breast height
or greater that are retained count toward the requirement at 1.5 times their projected
canopy area.

## § 59-6.4.1 Specimen tree removal

Removal of a specimen tree, defined as any tree of 30 inch diameter at breast height or
greater, requires a tree removal permit and replacement at a ratio of 3 caliper inches
planted per 10 caliper inches removed. Removal of a specimen tree without a permit is
subject to penalty under § 59-10.2.1.

## § 59-7.1.1 Stormwater management, applicability

Any development that creates 5,000 square feet or more of new impervious area, or that
disturbs one-half acre or more of land, shall submit a stormwater management plan prepared
by a licensed professional engineer. Redevelopment that reduces existing impervious area
is exempt from the water quantity requirement but remains subject to water quality
treatment under § 59-7.2.3.

## § 59-7.2.3 Stormwater quality treatment

A stormwater management plan shall provide water quality treatment for the first one inch
of runoff from all new impervious area. Treatment shall be achieved through infiltration,
bioretention, or an approved equivalent practice. Underground detention alone does not
satisfy the water quality requirement.

## § 59-7.3.1 Floodplain overlay

No structure shall be erected within the mapped 100 year floodplain except as permitted
under the Floodplain Overlay provisions of Article 7. Where construction is permitted, the
lowest floor including basement shall be elevated to not less than 2 feet above the base
flood elevation.

## § 59-7.4.2 Erosion and sediment control

Land disturbance of 2,500 square feet or more requires an approved erosion and sediment
control plan. Controls shall be installed before any clearing or grading begins and
maintained until permanent stabilization is achieved.

## § 59-8.1.1 Nonconforming structures

A structure lawfully existing on the effective date of this Title that does not conform to
the dimensional standards of its district may be maintained and repaired. It shall not be
enlarged in a manner that increases the extent of the nonconformity. A nonconforming
structure damaged to less than 60 percent of its assessed value may be restored to its
previous dimensions within twenty-four months of the damage.

## § 59-8.2.1 Nonconforming lots of record

A lot of record that does not meet the minimum lot area or width of its district may be
developed with a single family detached dwelling provided that all setback requirements
are met and lot coverage does not exceed the district maximum.

## § 59-9.1.1 Variances, standard of review

The Board of Zoning Appeals may grant a variance from the dimensional standards of this
Title only where the applicant demonstrates that strict application would result in
unnecessary hardship arising from conditions peculiar to the property, that the hardship
was not self-created, and that the variance will not be contrary to the public interest.
Economic disadvantage alone does not constitute unnecessary hardship.

## § 59-9.2.1 Variances, use variances prohibited

No variance shall be granted to permit a use not otherwise allowed in the district. A
request to permit an otherwise prohibited use shall be processed as a rezoning under
Article 11.

## § 59-10.2.1 Penalties

A violation of this Title is a civil offense subject to a penalty of not more than $1,000
per day of violation. Each day a violation continues constitutes a separate offense. The
City may in addition seek injunctive relief and may issue a stop-work order under
§ 59-10.3.1.

## § 59-10.3.1 Stop-work orders

The Director may issue a stop-work order where work is proceeding without a required
permit, in a manner that departs materially from approved plans, or in a manner that
presents an immediate hazard. No permit shall be issued for a parcel subject to an open
stop-work order until the order has been lifted.
